# UK Attention Atlas — pilot plan

**Purpose and scope.** Build a UK research and monitoring tool connecting news, search and social attention with weather, economic conditions and everyday disruption. Start with **climate change, cost of living, and clean transport**, keeping **EVs as a separately selectable subtopic**. Target history from 2022 where available, plus scheduled updates; audit coverage before committing to a common period. Begin with English-language coverage and explicitly assess Welsh-language gaps. Prioritise reproducible publication graphics and descriptive comparisons; causal attribution and predictive modelling follow validation.

**Datasets and measurement**

| Layer | Proposed sources and pilot decisions |
| --- | --- |
| **News and outlets** | Extend GDELT Web NGrams/GAL collection to retain UK article URLs, dates, titles, outlets and topic evidence. Also record the **whole UK GDELT article universe** for each day (all identifiable articles from the agreed UK outlet universe), so topic counts can be reported as a percentage of all captured news. Build a reviewed outlet registry: ownership, national/regional/local scope, coverage areas, and evidence-backed political leaning, with dates and “unknown/mixed” categories. Treat leaning as an outlet attribute, not each article’s stance. Audit missing local publishers. [GDELT](https://blog.gdeltproject.org/announcing-the-new-web-news-ngrams-3-0-dataset/) |
| **Search and social** | Apply under T&E for the access-limited **Google Trends API**; collect UK topic/search series, including air-conditioning queries, with supported subnational breakdowns. Start social collection with a defined UK-associated Bluesky account/topic panel; X depends on access and budget. Record posts, authors and engagement separately; do not infer UK residency from English text. Social backfills and geography remain coverage-dependent. [Google](https://developers.google.com/search/apis/trends), [Bluesky](https://github.com/bluesky-social/bsky-docs/blob/main/docs/advanced-guides/firehose.mdx) |
| **Weather, vegetation and hazards** | **HadUK-Grid** daily temperature/rainfall and local seasonal anomalies; provisional releases arrive after month-end, so timely monitoring needs a separately assessed observation feed. Use cloud-screened **Sentinel-2 NDVI** for local greenness, with a grassland mask before calling it grass condition. Retain GDACS/FIRMS/MODIS; supplement with national flood-agency data. Warning areas, hotspots and observed flooded/burned footprints must remain distinct. [Met Office](https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/research/climate/maps-and-data/data/haduk-grid/overview), [Copernicus](https://documentation.dataspace.copernicus.eu/APIs/SentinelHub/Process/Examples/S2L2A.html), [EA](https://environment.data.gov.uk/flood-monitoring/doc/reference) |
| **Prices and household pressures** | **EIA Brent**, **DESNZ weekly pump prices**, **ONS monthly inflation**, and selected stock/index prices from a licensed feed chosen with T&E. Air-conditioner searches enter immediately when Trends access permits; retail prices require a defined product basket and prospective retailer/partner collection, with historical availability unproven. [EIA](https://www.eia.gov/dnav/pet/hist/LeafHandler.ashx?f=M&n=PET&s=RBRTE), [DESNZ](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/weekly-road-fuel-prices), [ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/datasets/consumerpriceindices/current) |
| **Disruption and dated events** | **Darwin/Network Rail** delays and cancellations for selected GB routes, with Northern Ireland coverage assessed separately; Darwin’s advertised historical performance window is up to one year. Heat attribution requires incident/cause evidence. Add a sourced calendar of policies, elections, launches and energy shocks. [National Rail](https://www.nationalrail.co.uk/developers/darwin-data-feeds/) |

**Article analysis.** Store daily UK totals and report each topic as a percentage of **all GDELT-captured articles in the defined UK outlet universe**, as well as percentages within relevant subsets. Match numerator/denominator dates, outlets and analyzable coverage; publish the denominator definition alongside every chart. Collect wildfire and fuel-price article sets independently of climate/EV matches, enabling “wildfire articles mentioning climate change” and “fuel-price articles mentioning EVs” percentages. Deduplicate URLs, flag syndication, retain classifier versions, and review precision/recall samples. Full-text analysis requires a separate permitted-access workflow; unavailable articles remain unknown, not negative matches. This denominator describes the captured source universe, not all UK journalism.

**Two main screens.** **UK map:** counties and local-authority equivalents across all four nations, using versioned [ONS geography](https://www.ons.gov.uk/methodology/geography/ukgeographies/administrativegeography/ourchanginggeography/localgovernmentrestructuring); selectable temperature, vegetation and hazard layers. Distinguish outlet coverage, article-mentioned places and event location; national news is not county audience attention. **Attention timeline:** combine topics, channels, outlet filters, geographic selections, prices and physical measures; add event markers, smoothing, lag views and article drill-down. Offer raw units, clearly labelled standardisation or separate aligned panels; preserve weekly/monthly resolution and missingness. Export figures, data and saved specifications.

**Architecture and operation.** The repository and deployment should be owned by **T&E**, with T&E-controlled cloud billing, credentials and data retention. Two viable choices are:

1. **GCP-native (recommended for the pilot):** Cloud Run jobs + Cloud Scheduler, Cloud Storage for versioned Parquet/raw responses, BigQuery for NGrams and denominator queries, and Cloud SQL/PostGIS or BigQuery GIS for UK geography and serving. This gives one IAM, billing, logging and scheduled-job boundary; the current React app can still be served from Cloud Storage/CDN or Cloud Run.
2. **Hybrid (least migration):** keep React/Netlify and Supabase/PostGIS for serving, while T&E owns GCP BigQuery, Cloud Storage and scheduled Cloud Run jobs for collection and analysis. This is quicker to adopt but leaves two permission, monitoring and release systems.

Either option requires replacing fixed topic/hazard assumptions, fixing taxonomy-update merging, authenticated pilot access, restrictive database policies, and one validated dataset version across both screens. Schedule news/search/social refreshes daily where supported, economic feeds at publication frequency, and satellite/weather updates when released; add retries, overlapping refresh windows, completeness alerts, freshness labels, backups, secrets management and scan-budget caps.

**Indicative BigQuery cost.** The existing project dry-run scanned **587.958 GB for four themes across all countries for July 2026** (and 1,080.384 GB when run as separate topic jobs). A seven-day batch is approximately 7/31 of that: about **133 GB**, or roughly **US$0.75–$0.85 per run** at BigQuery’s published **$6.25/TiB on-demand rate**, before the monthly 1 TiB free tier ([pricing](https://cloud.google.com/bigquery/pricing)). With three similarly sized topics rather than four, a rough starting estimate is **US$0.55–$0.65 per weekly run**. That is approximately **US$30–$45 per year** after applying the same simple scaling, and likely zero query charge if the project remains below the monthly free tier. These are planning estimates, not quotes: UK filters do not guarantee proportionally cheaper scans, denominator/GAL queries may be materially larger, and retries, backfills, article exports, storage, Cloud Run, egress and third-party feeds are additional. Run a non-billable dry run for the exact UK taxonomy, denominator and overlap window before approving a budget.

**Delivery gates.** (1) Audit access, coverage and scan costs; agree topics, outlet labels and local pilot areas. (2) Deliver news denominators, article analysis, both screens and automated core inputs. (3) Integrate validated search/social and disruption feeds as access permits. Acceptance: two reproducible case studies—heat/disruption and fuel-price/EV attention—with reviewed classifications, reliable refreshes and exportable charts. T&E supplies account ownership, access applications, review support and budget approval.
